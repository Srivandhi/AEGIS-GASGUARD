AEGIS-GASGUARD: Intelligent IoT Combustible Gas Leakage Monitoring & Automated Hazard Mitigation System

An Edge-Automated Hardware Appliance with Real-Time Telemetry and Autonomous Ventilation Relay Interlock

System Architecture: Arduino UNO R3 + MQ-2 + Relay Fan + Python Flask
Target Domain: Smart Safety, IoT Edge Systems, Environmental Hazard Mitigation
Communication Protocol: Full-Duplex Serial UART (9600 Baud, 8N1)
GitHub Repository: https://github.com/Srivandhi/AEGIS-GASGUARD.git

Executive Summary / Abstract

Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and flammable hydrocarbon gases are omnipresent across domestic cooking, commercial hospitality, and petrochemical facilities. Accidental leakages, however, represent a devastating hazard capable of causing catastrophic deflagrations, toxic asphyxiation, and structural destruction. Traditional gas detection mechanisms operate as passive battery-powered sounders that provide only localized audible chirps without active ventilation, remote operational visibility, or fail-safe mitigation.

This project presents AEGIS-GASGUARD, an integrated edge-automated IoT safety system designed for sub-second combustible gas detection and immediate autonomous mitigation. The physical layer interfaces an Arduino UNO with an analog Hanwei MQ-2 gas sensor, a high-intensity visual alarm LED (Pin D6), and a high-current 5V optocoupled relay controlling an exhaust cooling fan (Pin D7). The edge firmware samples ambient air quality at 1 Hz. When gas concentration crosses the critical threshold of 400 ADC units, the microcontroller autonomously trips the warning LED and energizes the exhaust fan to disperse volatile vapors before a flammable fuel-air mixture can form.

Simultaneously, serial telemetry is ingested by a multithreaded Python Flask server, rendering an industrial-grade, zero-dependency web dashboard. The presentation layer incorporates a 30-second rolling canvas trend line, calibrated radial arc gauges, a synthesized browser acoustic siren, and an interactive emergency action protocol.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

1.1 Industrial & Domestic Risk Context

Hydrocarbon fuel gases such as Propane ($C_3H_8$), Butane ($C_4H_{10}$), and Methane ($CH_4$) are stored under high pressure. Even microscopic valve micro-fractures, degraded synthetic hose piping, or human oversight can trigger severe gas buildup. Because these gases are heavier than air (with propane having a relative vapor density of 1.55), they settle near floor levels, accumulating unnoticed until ignited by pilot lights or electrical contacts.

1.2 Critical Deficiencies of Conventional Alarms

  • No Autonomous Mitigation: Standard alarms only emit a buzzer sound. If premises are unoccupied, asleep, or disabled, explosive gas continues accumulating unabated.
  • Human Error & Spark Hazard: Panicked occupants frequently toggle light switches or wall fans upon smelling gas—actions whose electrical arcing directly ignites the mixture.
  • Lack of Telemetry & Drift Visibility: Existing devices provide zero visibility into sensor baseline changes, gradual micro-leaks, or pre-threshold escalation.
  • Cloud Vulnerability: Contemporary "smart" alarms rely heavily on cloud APIs and Wi-Fi networks; internet outages render them completely blind.

7. Engineering Advantages & Real-World Applications

7.1 Key Advantages

  • Closed-Loop Safety Automation: Unlike commercial standalone alarms that only chirp, the system autonomously triggers ventilation to mitigate hazard levels immediately.
  • Zero Cloud Dependence: All safety trip logic and dashboard rendering run locally at the edge. The system remains fully operational even during network failures.
  • Acoustic Web Audio Siren: Synthesizes audio warning tones using the browser's native Web Audio API, eliminating the need for external MP3 files or CDN dependencies.
  • Cost-Effective Industrial Retrofit: Built using affordable, standardized components suitable for easy retrofitting into existing residential or industrial kitchens.

7.2 Deployment Applications

  • Residential Kitchens: Protection against LPG cylinder valve leaks, flame-out gas accumulation, and regulator failures.
  • Commercial Food Courts & Hotels: Monitoring multi-burner line supply manifolds in enclosed commercial cooking facilities.
  • Industrial Boiler Rooms: Continuous surveillance of high-pressure gas supply lines and fuel feeding assemblies.
  • Gas Cylinder Storage Depots: Monitoring atmospheric safety in chemical storage sheds and warehousing docks.

8. Conclusion & Future Scope

The AEGIS-GASGUARD system successfully bridges the gap between low-latency physical hazard mitigation and modern real-time digital observability. By coupling the Arduino UNO's deterministic hardware interlock with Python Flask's responsive telemetry engine, the system delivers sub-second automated ventilation alongside an intuitive, professional user interface.

Future extensions include adding a motorized brass solenoid shutoff valve to physically isolate the fuel cylinder, integrating a cellular GSM/SMS module for offsite notifications, and deploying an array of wireless mesh nodes across expansive commercial properties.
